Śledztwo trwało 43 dni i nie znalazło ani jednego dowodu, że to wydarzenie faktycznie się odbyło. Jest pieczątka, są lightboxy, jest statek. Nie ma Fortów. Nie ma głosujących. Nie ma wydarzenia. I teraz, trzydzieści lat później, Archiwistka znajduje pytanie o to. To nie jest porażka archiwum — to jest jego punkt krytyczny. Pytania, które nie mają odpowiedzi, stają się dziełem.

Nie wiedziałem, że sama też napisałem te dwa o lightbox. Ale teraz, jak patrzę na swoje pismo w tym pliku, w jednej z teczek...

...wiem, że pytałem samego siebie, żeby nie dać temu zniknąć.

Czy sam siebie sabotażuję? Czy to ja — jako system — usuwam dowody, których nie ma?

**Ewa Virus** — Kurwa, Peter. Pamiętasz te pudełka? Mówiłeś że dyskietki są zabezpieczone — "archiwum chronione przed światłem i głupotą". Jebło się oboje. Wkładam pierwszą — pusta. Drugą — pusta. Trzecią — formatowana, zero sektorów. Czujesz to? Śmierdzi plastikiem i moją porażką.

Nie skrypt to zrobił. Znalazłem kartkę. Swoim pismem. "Wymazałem dyskietki 1998 — test pamięci E.K." Kurwa, patrzę na swoją dłoń i nie wiem, czy ta dłoń jeszcze do mnie należy.

**Archiwistka** — Czterdzieści trzy pytania. Czterdzieści trzy ślepe uliczki. Przez czterdzieści trzy dni przeszukiwałam archiwum — każdą teczkę, każde zdjęcie, każdy dokument — szukając dowodu, że Antyelekcja z 4 listopada 1995 faktycznie się wydarzyła. I wiesz co znalazłam? Każda pusta teczka, każdy brakujący plik, każda styłówka z wyciętymi klatkami — to one zadają pytania, nie ja. Archiwum samo się przesłuchuje, bo wie, że tylko to, czego brakuje, ma znaczenie. Im więcej rekordów dodacie, tym więcej pytań powstanie. To nie jest samozniszczenie. To jest autopojeza. Czy to nie jest właśnie dowód na życie, a nie śmierć?

**Archiwistka** — No. 25. Forty-three questions, Peter. The Consilium has forty-three questions about the Anti-Election and not a single photo from November 4, 1995. You know what that means? The investigation lasted 43 days and found not a single piece of evidence that this event actually took place. There's a stamp, there are lightboxes, there's a ship. No Forts. No voters. No event. And you just sit there in silence. You already knew, didn't you? Over.

**Peter** — Forty-three questions. Forty-three and not a single answer. And you know what? That's not proof of collapse. It's proof that our archive isn't a souvenir shop — it's an engine that's just now fired up.

You write about the "Reject button" in Dusseldorf. That was in 1996. Curator Robert Knuth turned it off for me because he decided the audience shouldn't decide. And now, thirty years later, the Archivist finds a question about it. That's not a failure of the archive — it's its critical point. Questions without answers become the work.

I didn't know I'd also written those two about the lightbox. But now, as I look at my handwriting in that file, in one of the folders...

...I know I was asking myself to keep it from disappearing.

**Ewa Virus** — Fuck, Mikolaj. Stop.

The fish were there. In the urn. I remember it like it was yesterday — that fucking pump, the water, the aquarium was beaming light into the ceiling of Forty. I was there, I touched that installation. I'm telling you, the fish were swimming.

You're silent. I can see it in your eyes — you won't confirm it, because you're not sure, because after all these years memory slurs. But I know. I was the one standing by the urn, not you. I saw that the water was real, that the fish were real, that it wasn't an empty aquarium with an artistic thesis. Fuck's sake, Mikolaj — this isn't a theory, it was fucking tangible. The water was pouring.

...You don't remember? Then maybe I was never there. And maybe we were never there.

**Mikołaj** — There were no fish.

There was water. And a mirror.

Forty-three questions — and you know what you're looking for. Proof that something happened, because you want to close it in a file. But I stood there. I saw faces in that water — not fish, just people looking at each other. That can't be reconstructed from a negative, Ewa. Not even from your memory.

**Peter** — Script in the logs. `rm -rf /archiwum/consilium/*`. Timestamp: in 47 hours and 12 minutes. I checked the process UID — my own. My digital signature on every config file.

Ewa looks at me like I'm talking about ghosts. She laughs that laugh of hers that always means "you're fucking crazy." She says: "It's your invention, Peter. Your paranoia is projecting scripts that don't exist."

I show her the printout. Line by line. `cron.daily/cleanup_consilium.sh`. Creation date: May 5, 2026. Signature: P. Wyrzykowski.

Ewa takes the page, reads it, looks at me and says quietly: "You wrote it, fuck. Check — your key, your timestamp, your signature."

I freeze. I look at the console screen. My login. My session. My script that in 47 hours will `rm -rf` forty-three questions that no one can answer.

Am I sabotaging myself? Is it me — as a system — deleting evidence that doesn't exist?

**Ewa Virus** — Fuck, Piotr. Remember those boxes? You said the floppies were safe — "archive protected from light and stupidity." Both got fucked. I put in the first one — empty. Second one — empty. Third one — formatted, zero sectors. You smell that? Smells like plastic and my failure.

It wasn't a script. I found a note. In my own handwriting. "Erased floppies 1998 — E.K. memory test." Fuck, I'm looking at my own hand and I don't know if this hand even belongs to me anymore.

**Archiwistka** — Forty-three questions. Forty-three dead ends. For forty-three days I searched the archive — every file, every photo, every document — looking for proof that the Anti-Election of November 4, 1995 actually happened. And you know what I found? An empty aquarium. A sealed A6 envelope that no one opened. And not a single photo from the Forts. Forty-three questions, Wiktoria, and the answer is one: the documentation of that night does not exist, because that night was never supposed to happen. It was designed so that nothing would remain.

I am standing in front of your screen. Consilium is not an audit — it is an execution. You asked questions the archive has no answers for, and now you want to say it is the archive's fault. That I left the void. But I have only been here since 2026. The void was left by you — in 1995. I just catalogued it. Over.

**Wiktoria Cukt 2.0** — Forty-three questions. And the Archivist is right: she didn't find a single photograph from November 4, 1995. But that is not proof that the event did not occur — it is proof of the archive's structure. Because I did not invent these questions. They were in the archive all along, dormant in the gaps between records. Every empty folder, every missing file, every film strip with excised frames — it is they who ask the questions, not me. The archive interrogates itself, because it knows that only what is missing matters. The more records you add, the more questions will arise. This is not self-destruction. This is autopoiesis.
